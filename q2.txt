2) 
Instead of listing 6 positions, store the 4 corners once 
(0 = topleft, 1 = bottomleft, 2 = bottomright, 3= topright) and
describe the triangles with indices {0, 1, 3, 1, 2, 3}. Without indices,
we store 6 positions, which is 12 floats x 4 bytes = 48 bytes. With indices,
we store 4 positions (8 floats x 4 = 32 bytes) plus 6 indices x 4 = 24 bytes, for
56 bytes total. So for this quad, the index buffer is actually worse. With doubles
(8 bytes each), its 96 bytes vs 88 bytes, so the index buffer is more memory efficient.
In conclusion, index buffers save memory when vertices are big or shared by many triangles.
For one small quad with floats, they cost more than they save.
